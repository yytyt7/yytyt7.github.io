## 🖥️ What porthole Does

porthole is a control panel for SSH port forwards. An SSH port forward creates a secure path between your computer and another machine. For example, a forward can make a service on a remote machine appear as a local port on your own computer. When the forward is on, the local port accepts connections and passes them through the secure tunnel. When the forward is off, that path is closed.

Managing these forwards usually means remembering commands and checking whether a tunnel is already running. porthole replaces that with a list. Each tunnel has a name or label, a visible state, and a control for switching it on or off. You can see at a glance whether a tunnel is on, off, starting, or stopping.

The second job is the port view. This list shows the ports listening on your machine. It answers simple questions such as whether a local service is running, which port it is using, and whether an old service is still open. Together, the tunnel list and the port list give you a complete picture of your connections in one place.

## ❓ Troubleshooting

If the panel does not show what you expect, start with the refresh control. It rereads the current state and often clears up a stale view.

If a tunnel will not switch on, check its state first. A tunnel that is already starting may need a moment to finish. If it stays in a starting state, switch it off, wait, and try again.

If a port you expect does not appear, confirm that the tunnel is on and refresh the list. If the port still does not appear, the service behind the tunnel may not be running.

If the widget is not visible in the tray, open your KDE Plasma 6 system tray settings and make sure porthole is allowed to show there. If the panel looks empty, refresh once and check again.

## 🛡️ Privacy and Safety

porthole runs on your own machine and shows information about your own connections. It does not send your tunnel details or port list anywhere else. Treat the panel as a private view of your computer's networking state, and switch tunnels off when they are not needed.
